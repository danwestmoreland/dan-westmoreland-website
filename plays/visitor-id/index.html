The visitor-ID play that emailed you.

If you got here from an email that said "you were on danwestmoreland.com last week," this is the system that sent it. A visit to my site became a company name, a Slack post, a HubSpot record, and a three-email sequence, and nobody touched it. Here is the whole build, with screenshots of the live version.

For: B2B software teams with a website, a CRM, and no SDRStack: RB2B, Slack, Make, HubSpot, Smartlead, ClayCost: about $50 a month, $200 with ClayBuild time: two afternoons, then an hour a week

What it does

A script on the site identifies the company behind a visit, and often the person. That hit posts to a Slack channel. An automation reads the channel four times a day, checks the company against a list of 2,600 companies that fit the work I do, and does one of three things: stamps the matching HubSpot record with the visit, creates a new record and marks it a good fit, or creates a record and marks it no fit so I never see it again. Good fits with an email go into a sequence that says exactly what just happened. The email you got is email one of three.

Every piece is a product you can buy today, and four of the five are free at my volume. The play is the wiring, and the wiring is below.

The build in six steps

1. IdentifyRB2B script on the site, alerts to Slack
2. ParseMake reads the post and pulls out domain, person, page
3. MatchSearch HubSpot for the domain in the list
4. TriageNo match? Score the firmographics in the post
5. StampWrite the visit to the company and contact
6. SequenceGood fits with an email go to Smartlead
  1. Put the visitor-ID script on the site and point the alerts at Slack. RB2B gives you one tag. I gate it to the production hostname so preview deploys do not burn the free identifications. Create a channel for the alerts and nothing else; the automation reads that channel and does not want noise in it.
  2. Parse the Slack post with a regex, not a parser you pay for. RB2B posts in two shapes, person-level and company-level, and one pattern with an optional person block handles both. Out come name, title, company, email, LinkedIn, the page visited, and the domain. Make's text parser does this for one operation.
  3. Search your CRM for the domain. The list of 2,600 fit companies lives in HubSpot as company records with the domain as the unique key. One search per visit tells you whether this is a company you already decided you want.
  4. If there is no match, triage on what the post already contains. RB2B includes employee band, industry, and revenue band. A router sends software and internet companies with 11 to 200 people and revenue over $1M down the good-fit route; everything else gets a record marked no fit, which costs one operation and means I never evaluate it twice. The rule lives in two filters you can read in the screenshot.
  5. Stamp the record. Last visit date, last page, and the firmographics go on the company. If the post named a person with an email, the contact is created or updated with the same stamps and associated by domain. An active segment, "visited site," fills itself.
  6. Sequence the good fits. A weekly export of the good-fit segment goes into a Smartlead campaign on three warmed inboxes. Email one names the visit and explains this system. Email two links here. Email three gives the free version below. Replies come to me; "nope" ends it; "plays" gets the newsletter instead.

What breaks

The free identification quota. 150 a month sounds like a lot until a bot crawls your site. Gate the script to real pages on the production domain and watch the count in the first week.

The post format changes. The whole chain hangs on a regex against a Slack message. When the tool changes a label, the parser returns nothing and the run looks fine. Make the parser continue on no match and route empty results to the no-fit record so you notice.

Company-level hits with no person. About half of hits are company only. Those need a person before they can get an email, which is the Clay step, and it is the only step that costs credits. Do it weekly in a batch, not per hit.

Writing to the CRM from an automation. HubSpot Starter needs the connected app granted write scope on companies before anything lands, and date fields want a timestamp, not a string. Both cost me an hour. Budget it.

The email itself. Naming the visit is direct, and a few people will not like it. The softer opener is "a company that looks a lot like yours was on my site last week." I chose the direct one because the people I want to work with are the ones who ask how it works.

The free version

You do not need the list, the CRM, or the sequencer to get most of the value. Put the visitor-ID script on your pricing page only. Send every hit to a Slack channel your sales team watches. That is one afternoon and $0, and in a month you will know whether the full build is worth it, because you will have a count of fit companies that read your pricing page and a record of what sales did about them.

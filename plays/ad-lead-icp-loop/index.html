The ad-lead ICP loop.

A LinkedIn lead-gen form lead gets a reply from a human inside five minutes if they fit, a nurture if they half fit, and nothing if they are a student who wanted the ebook. Then, every Friday, the same system scores each ad campaign by how many fits it produced and tells you which one to pause. The ads budget finally hears from the pipeline.

For: B2B software companies spending $2K or more a month on lead-gen form adsStack: HubSpot free, LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms, n8n, Apollo free, Cal.com, Smartlead, SlackCost: $39 to about $60/month on top of ad spendBuild time: a day, plus a week of watching it

The play

Lead-gen form ads are the cheapest leads you will ever buy and the slowest you will ever work. The form fills land in the ad platform, somebody exports them on Thursday, a rep emails on Monday, and the controller who downloaded your checklist a week ago has no idea who you are. At Curve Dental, speed-to-lead on inbound was the single biggest lever in taking demos from 60 a month to 200 and up. Ad leads are inbound. Nobody treats them that way.

The second problem is that nobody closes the loop. Marketing reports leads per campaign and cost per lead. Sales says the leads are junk. Both are right, because an ebook about fraud pulls in students and compliance officers at 10,000-person insurers, and a month-end checklist pulls in controllers at 600-person dental groups. Same budget, same dashboard, very different pipeline.

So this play does two things with one set of rules. Every new lead is scored against the ICP within minutes of arriving and routed by score. Every week, the scores roll up by campaign, and any campaign producing fits under a threshold gets flagged to pause. The ICP definition that routes the lead is the same one that grades the ad. That is the whole trick.

Who it's for

  • You run LinkedIn or Google lead-gen form campaigns and get at least 20 leads a week. Below that, a person can do this by hand and should.
  • You have an ICP you can write down in three lines: titles, company size, market. If "we sell to everyone" is the answer, fix that first.
  • Somebody owns the fast reply and has a calendar link. The five minutes only matter if the email comes from a person who will take the call.
  • Marketing and sales share a CRM. HubSpot free is enough. The point is one record with a campaign name and a fit score on it.

The build

1. LandLinkedIn Lead Gen Forms sync to HubSpot free as contacts with the campaign name on them
2. Polln8n checks for new paid-social contacts every five minutes
3. ScoreApollo fills title, size, industry; three yes/no checks give 0 to 3
4. Route3 gets a personal email with a booking link now; 1 to 2 goes to nurture; 0 stops
5. ReportFriday: fit rate and cost per fit lead by campaign, with pause flags, to Slack

Stack

CaptureHubSpot free, with LinkedIn Ads connected so lead-gen form fills become contacts carrying the campaign name. HubSpot's new Ads Leads tab (October 2026, on Marketing Hub Free) shows every form lead in one place and flags broken forms, which is where you check that step 1 is still working.
Schedule and routen8n, self-hosted (free) or Cloud Starter at about EUR 20/month. HubSpot free has no workflows, so n8n polls the contacts API on a schedule. The mock below runs the same rules in Python so you can read them.
EnrichApollo free for title, company size, industry, country. The free plan's credits cover a few hundred lookups a month; at higher volume, enrich only the leads whose title passes.
Fast replyGmail from the rep's own inbox, sent by n8n, with a Cal.com (free) link. Not a marketing email. One paragraph, no logo.
NurtureSmartlead ($39/month), one three-step campaign per offer, for the score 1 and 2 leads.
Reportn8n on a Friday schedule, posting the campaign table to a Slack (free) channel that the person who controls the ad budget actually reads.
  1. Connect the ads to the CRM. LinkedIn Ads to HubSpot is a native integration. Make sure the campaign name lands on the contact as a property, not just in the original source field. Everything downstream keys on it.
  2. Write the ICP as three checks. Title matches a buyer list and not an exclusion list. Company size inside a band. Industry and country in your market. Each check is one point. Put the lists in a table your team can edit without touching the workflow; in the mock it's a CSV.
  3. Poll, don't wait. n8n fetches contacts created in the last ten minutes with a paid-social source, every five minutes, and skips any contact that already has a score. The skip is what stops double-fires when HubSpot updates the record.
  4. Enrich only what you need. Title comes free from the form. Size, industry, and country come from Apollo by domain. If the title fails outright, you can skip the lookup and save the credit.
  5. Route by score. Three: a personal email from the rep inside five minutes, naming the asset they downloaded and offering a time. One or two: a Smartlead nurture built around the asset. Zero: tag it and stop. Students and 10,000-person companies do not get a sequence.
  6. Write the score back. Fit score, the checks that passed, and the route go on the contact. This is what makes the Friday report a one-line query instead of a project.
  7. Report by campaign on Friday. Leads, fits, fit rate, spend, and cost per fit lead, one row per campaign. Spend comes from a small table you update weekly or from the ads API when you get around to it.
  8. Flag the pause. Any campaign under 20 percent fit rate for two weeks running gets a PAUSE flag in the Slack post. The flag is a recommendation. A human pauses the campaign, and the threshold is a number in the rules table, not an opinion in a meeting.

What breaks

The sync is slower than your promise. LinkedIn to HubSpot can lag 10 to 30 minutes. Your five-minute reply is five minutes from when HubSpot sees the contact, not from the form fill. Say "a few minutes ago" in the email, never a number, and check the Leads tab for form issues weekly.

The poll fires twice. HubSpot updates a contact several times in its first minutes of life, and a naive "created in the last ten minutes" filter will pick it up on two polls. The fit score property is the lock: if it is already set, skip. Set it before you send, not after.

The fast reply reads like a sequence. If it has a logo, a signature block, and "I hope this finds you well," it is a marketing email and gets treated like one. One paragraph, names the asset, offers a time. From the rep's own inbox.

The exclusion list is too short. The first week's fit list will have a student with a grand title and a consultant who sells to your buyers. Every one of those is a word for the exclusion list. The list grows for a month and then stops.

Somebody argues with the pause. The fraud ebook campaign had the most leads and the lowest cost per lead, and the person who built it will defend it. The report shows cost per fit lead next to cost per lead so the argument is about which number matters, which is a better argument to have.

Copy it

Take the four files, export a week of your own form leads in the same columns, and run the script. Before you wire anything, the campaign report alone will tell you whether your ad budget is buying pipeline or downloads. Then build the poll in n8n, put the three checks in a table, and give the rep a Cal.com link.

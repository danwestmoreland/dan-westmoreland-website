The orphan-account sweep.

Every CRM has a few hundred company records with no deal, no owner, and nobody who was ever emailed. Visitor ID tools, conference imports, enrichment runs, and a rep who left in March all put them there. This play finds every one, checks whether it fits, finds one human to write to, says why you're writing, and archives the rest so the pipeline view stops lying.

For: B2B software companies with 500 or more companies in the CRM and no RevOps hireStack: HubSpot free, n8n, Apollo free, Smartlead, SlackCost: $39 to about $60/monthBuild time: one afternoon for the segments, one for the workflow

The play

Open your CRM and filter companies to the ones with no associated deals. Now add no associated contacts, or contacts with no logged activity. At most companies I've walked into, that is half the database or more. Those records got there honestly. A visitor ID tool created a company when someone read the pricing page. Marketing imported a conference list. Somebody ran an enrichment job on an ICP search. A rep built a territory and then quit. Every one of those was a reason to follow up, and none of them came with an owner.

Two things go wrong. The obvious one is that nobody ever emails these companies, and some of them were on your pricing page. The quieter one is that they make every count in the CRM wrong. "We have 1,400 target accounts" means 500 you work and 900 you don't, and every report built on the company object inherits the lie.

HubSpot made this a one-filter job the week of November 2: segments can now filter on records that have no associated object of a type, on the free tier. So the play is a weekly sweep. Find the orphans. Drop the ones that don't fit and mark them archived. For the ones that fit, find the one person who would buy, write a why-now from wherever the record came from, and send a short sequence. The CRM gets honest and the forgotten accounts get a first email.

Who it's for

  • You have at least 500 companies in the CRM. Under that, run the filter and work the list by hand in a week.
  • Companies got into your CRM from more than one place: a visitor ID tool, imports, enrichment, reps. The sweep is most useful when the "source" column tells a story, because the story becomes the email.
  • Nobody owns CRM hygiene. If you have a RevOps person who already does this, send them the script and buy them lunch.
  • You can write down who fits in three lines: size, industry, country. The archive step is only as good as that.

The build

1. FindHubSpot segments: no associated deals, plus no contacts or no contact activity
2. SkipAnything created in the last 30 days, and anything already swept
3. FitSize, industry, country. Fails get a "swept: archived" property and leave the view
4. Find the humanApollo lookup by domain and buyer title. Nobody found means retry next week
5. Write and sendOne why-now per source type, three-step sequence, Monday digest to Slack

Stack

FindHubSpot free. Three segments on the company object using the new "has no associated" operator: no deals; no contacts; contacts but none with an email or meeting logged. The operator blocks further conditions once selected, so the segments stay simple and the union happens in n8n.
Schedule and routen8n, self-hosted (free) or Cloud Starter at about EUR 20/month. One workflow, runs Monday morning, reads the segments through the API, writes two properties back: "swept" and "swept reason." The mock below runs the same logic in Python.
Find the humanApollo free, people search by company domain and a buyer-title list. A few hundred lookups a month on free, which is plenty when the fit check runs first.
SendSmartlead ($39/month), one campaign per source type, three steps over 12 days, the why-now as a custom field. Replies post back to HubSpot and create a deal.
ReportSlack free. One Monday message: how many orphans, how many sequenced, how many archived, how many replied. The archived count is the one leadership will care about once they see it.
  1. Count first. Build the no-deals segment and screenshot the number. That number, next to the total, is the slide that gets you the afternoon to do the rest.
  2. Define orphan precisely. No open deal, and either no contacts or no contact that was ever emailed or met. A company with a contact who got one email in 2024 is still an orphan. A company created in the last 30 days is not; somebody may be on it.
  3. Add the two properties. "Swept" (date) and "swept reason" (sequenced, archived, no buyer, too new). Everything else keys on these, and they are what keep the sweep from emailing the same company twice.
  4. Fit check before any lookup. Size band, industry list, country list. In the mock it's a CSV; in HubSpot it's three properties the enrichment already filled. Fails get archived with the reason written out in plain words, so a human can disagree later.
  5. Find one buyer, not five. Apollo by domain, title in your buyer list, verified email. If nothing comes back, mark "no buyer" and let next week's run try again. Do not fall back to a generic info@ address.
  6. Write the why-now from the source. Visitor ID: name the page and the month, and own the silence. Rep import: a colleague who moved on flagged you and never wrote. Conference list: we had your name a year ago and didn't use it. Enrichment run: no clever reason, you're exactly who we built this for. Honest beats clever every time, and these five templates cover every record.
  7. Send small and reply fast. Three steps over 12 days, a booking link in the second email, and a reply creates a deal with the old company record attached so the history is there.
  8. Report every Monday. Orphans found, sequenced, archived, replied, and the running total. After a month, "orphans found" should be a trickle, which means the sweep has become a filter on new records instead of an excavation.

What breaks

The segment limit. HubSpot free caps active lists. Three segments for one play is a lot of your allowance. Build them, pull the IDs into n8n once a week, and if you're at the cap, use one segment (no deals) and do the contact test in n8n from the API.

The operator stops you adding conditions. Once "has no associated" is picked, the segment takes no further filters. That is why the fit check lives in n8n and not in the segment. Don't fight it.

The source column is empty. If you can't tell where a record came from, you can't write the why-now, and "no clever reason" becomes every email. Backfill the source from the create date and whatever import or tool was running that week. It's a day, and it's the day that makes the emails good.

The archive offends someone. A founder will see a 3,800-person insurer archived and say "but that's a great logo." The reason is written in plain words on the record; change the rule or move the record by hand. The sweep isn't deleting anything.

Apollo credits. A few hundred free lookups a month runs out on a big first sweep. Fit check first, then lookups only on fits, then cap the first run at 100 a week until the backlog is gone.

You email the same company twice. Two orphan records for one company, from two sources, will both pass. Dedupe on domain before the lookup. The swept property is what keeps week two from repeating week one.
